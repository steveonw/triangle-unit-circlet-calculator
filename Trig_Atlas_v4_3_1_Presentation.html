Supported mathematics

One common angle θ; integer constants, +, −, ×, ÷, parentheses, integer powers −12…12. Divisors and negative-power bases may be nonzero rational trigonometric expressions. Original undefined subexpressions and all isolated denominator roots are retained. The general engine is bounded to degree 48, coefficient size and work limits, and at most 32 distinguishable exclusions per period; overly complex inputs are rejected. Exact Sturm root counts isolate real exclusions; non-certified angles and finite nonrational limits are approximations. Selecting a listed root uses its numerical representative as an excluded angle. Typing a nearby rounded angle need not select the root. The older Laurent fast path remains unchanged. A defined base raised to zero is 1, including 0⁰; undefined bases remain undefined. sin^-1 means reciprocal power, not arcsine. Multiplication/division are left-to-right. Numeric readouts use local half-angle rational charts, exact cancellation at cardinal angles, and a binary-polynomial fallback. The final value is floating point, not arbitrary-precision trigonometry.

New / Three-circle experiment

One angle, three planes, one equilateral triangle

All circles have radius 1 and share the origin. Set c = cos θ and s = sin θ. The third circle uses a cyclic orientation, so P = (c,s,0), Q = (0,c,s), R = (s,0,c). The vertex labels still represent reciprocal functions, not coordinate signs or values.

Signed distance of plane from origin—(cos θ + sin θ) / √3
Triangle area—√3 / 4 × side²
Plane of triangle: x + y + z = cos θ + sin θ

Use Circles only to isolate the geometry. Turn on Tangent directions to see the direction of each derivative, a quarter-turn within its own plane. The arrows are scaled for visibility; the velocity triangle shows the derivative vectors themselves. A line that looks crossed in projection need not intersect in 3D. The purple function pairs also group the usual derivative and antiderivative rules.

How to read this mental model

Vertices: six functions. Axes: reciprocal pairs multiply to 1. Edges: all twelve edges represent products. Colors group related derivative / Pythagorean, quotient, and sum or difference identities. Faces: two opposite faces multiply to 1; the other six multiply to the square of one of their vertices.

All identities hold on their common domains. At a denominator zero, the reciprocal or quotient is undefined; vertical dashed lines show its asymptotes. Sine and cosine have period 2π and amplitude 1. Tangent and cotangent have period π; secant and cosecant have period 2π. The four unbounded functions have no finite amplitude. None has a horizontal asymptote.

This is a constructed mnemonic, not a literal geometric definition of the functions. The octahedron stays regular while the shared angle changes the function values. The derivative overlay represents slope with respect to radians, even though the slider is labeled in degrees.
