New / Three-circle experiment

One angle, three planes, one equilateral triangle

All circles have radius 1 and share the origin. Set c = cos θ and s = sin θ. The third circle uses a cyclic orientation, so P = (c,s,0), Q = (0,c,s), R = (s,0,c). The vertex labels still represent reciprocal functions, not coordinate signs or values.

Signed distance of plane from origin—(cos θ + sin θ) / √3
Triangle area—√3 / 4 × side²
Plane of triangle: x + y + z = cos θ + sin θ

Use Circles only to isolate the geometry. Turn on Tangent vectors to see the derivative of each moving radius as a quarter-turn within its own plane. A line that looks crossed in projection need not intersect in 3D.

Six functions: derivative and integral readout

Definite integral from starting angle a to current angle θ. If an interval crosses or touches a pole, the ordinary integral is undefined or divergent; cancellation across a pole is not used.

How to read this mental model

Vertices: six functions. Axes: reciprocal pairs multiply to 1. Edges: three Pythagorean / derivative pairings, six product identities, three sum or difference identities. Faces: two opposite faces multiply to 1; the other six multiply to the square of one of their vertices.

All identities hold on their common domains. At a denominator zero, the reciprocal or quotient is undefined; vertical dashed lines show its asymptotes. Sine and cosine have period 2π and amplitude 1. Tangent and cotangent have period π; secant and cosecant have period 2π. The four unbounded functions have no finite amplitude. None has a horizontal asymptote.

This is a constructed mnemonic, not a literal geometric definition of the functions. The octahedron stays regular while the shared angle changes the function values. The derivative overlay represents slope with respect to radians, even though the slider is labeled in degrees.
